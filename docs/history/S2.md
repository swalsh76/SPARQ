# S2 Weather against station observations (closed 2026-10-28)

S2 asked whether a taker can buy the certain side of a weather market after the station reports prove it. The book priced the bound hours before the reports, so no rule was proposed (W9; [weather-sources.md](../research/weather-sources.md), [weather-explore.md](../research/weather-explore.md)).

## The plan as approved (PLAN §6.2)

**Why this strand (the §9 reassessment, 2026-10-27).**
- K0.5 showed Kalshi's prices are close to the true probability.
- S1 showed that plain spread capture is closed to a late joiner: in S1.2a, a joining order's fills lose 2.9 cents by 10 minutes before fees.
- So an edge needs a fair value better than the book's, from information the book is slow to price.

Kalshi's daily temperature and rain markets settle on a station's measurement, and the station's own reports during the day put **hard bounds** on that measurement:
- once the reported temperature has passed a bracket's top, that bracket cannot win;
- once measurable rain is reported, "rain today" is settled in practice.

The question is whether the book still offers prices on the wrong side of a bound after the report is public: how often, for how long, in what size, and what that is worth after fees. It is the cheapest strand to test, because the 77 days of trades are already on the analytics node and the observation archives are free.

**What the history holds** (2026-08-07 to 2026-10-22, read on 2026-10-27 for this proposal; no outcome was measured). Climate and Weather has 458 series and 11.1 M trades, 1.4% of all trades. The 25 busiest series hold 89% of the weather contracts.
- **Rain.** `KXRAIN` is the busiest weather series, with 2.4 M trades (about 31,000 a day). It is one event a day with about 30 city markets. A market settles YES when precipitation at the climate station is strictly greater than 0 inches; a trace counts as 0.
- **Daily highs.** About 20 cities, each with six markets a city-day (two tails and four 2-degree brackets):
  - the busiest: `KXHIGHLAX` (1.1 M trades), `KXHIGHNY` (0.6 M), `KXHIGHMIA`, `KXHIGHCHI` and `KXHIGHAUS`;
  - then `KXHIGHTPHX`, `KXHIGHTSFO`, `KXHIGHTDAL`, `KXHIGHTSEA`, `KXHIGHTATL`, `KXHIGHPHIL`, `KXHIGHDEN`, `KXHIGHTHOU`, `KXHIGHTLV`, `KXHIGHTBOS`, `KXHIGHTOKC`, `KXHIGHTMIN`, `KXHIGHTDC` and smaller ones.

  A few cities also have daily lows.
- **Settlement.** The highs and rain now settle on **The Weather Company's** value for a named NWS climate station (`CLINYC`, `CLIMDW`, `CLILAX` and so on). Markets of December 2025 named the NWS Climatological Report (Daily) instead. The **hourly** temperature series (`KXTEMP...H`, `...HS`) settle on Synoptic Data's reading at a set hour.
- **Times.** A daily market opens the day before and closes at about 01:00 local time after its day, so it trades through the whole observed day.

**Decisions proposed (W1 to W8).**

| # | Decision |
|---|---|
| W1 | **Scope: the daily-high markets and `KXRAIN`.** Daily lows follow only if the highs show an edge. The hourly temperature series are **out**: their value is one reading at a set hour, so any edge is a race to see the reading first, which a feed arriving 0.55 s late (S1.2a) would lose. Taking stale quotes is tested first. Quoting around an observation-based fair value is a later slice, only if S2.3 passes. |
| W2 | **Ground truth is Kalshi's settled result,** not a weather source. Each test is scored on the market's own result, so a disagreement between the observations and The Weather Company shows up as a loss, not as an excluded case. |
| W3 | **Observations: the NWS's hourly and special station reports (METAR)** for each climate station. The history comes from the Iowa Environmental Mesonet's archive, and live use from NOAA's aviation weather service; both are free. The station map (series, climate code, station identifier, time zone) is stored as data and checked against each series' rules text. |
| W4 | **When a report counts: from its observation time plus a publication delay.** The delay is set from a live measurement in S2.1, and is never below 5 minutes for the history. **The bound is conservative:**<ul><li>reported temperatures, converted from tenths of a degree Celsius, are rounded down;</li><li>a bracket is ruled out only when the bound clears its top by at least 1 °F (the climate value is a whole °F from finer data, and its maximum can only be higher than any hourly report);</li><li>rain counts only from a report of at least 0.01 inch.</li></ul>Both margins are fixed before S2.2's results are seen; afterwards they may be widened, never narrowed. **Set 2026-10-28 (owner): the history delay is 10 minutes after the observation time** (S2.1's live day: first seen median 3.2 minutes, p95 5.2; 7 of 1,067 reports later than 10 minutes). |
| W5 | **Fills.** The history shows only trades, so a rule may take at most the size that actually traded on the wrong side of the bound after the report counted, at that trade's price or one worse for us. In the live run, at most the size shown. Taker fees follow Kalshi's schedule (KAPS D13), rounded up per order. |
| W6 | **Parts:**<ul><li>fit, 2026-08-07 to 2026-09-28, and validate, 2026-09-29 to 2026-10-10: explored freely;</li><li>test, 2026-10-11 to 2026-10-22: opened once.</li></ul>The unit, and the bootstrap's cluster, is the **city-day**, since a `KXRAIN` event spans 30 cities. *Caveat:* K0.5's Weather segments were scored on these test days, but as price-only rules; nothing about observations was read there. |

| W9 | **S2.2's outcome: no rules (owner, 2026-10-28).** On the fit part, trades a taker could have taken after the call reached 19 of 2,240 called markets: $41 a day at the upper bound (every such trade) and $0.65 at the lower (the first worth taking per market), against W7's $25; on the validate part, one trade and $0. The book had already priced the bound, with the certain side last below 95 cents a median 4.6 hours before the call for the highs and 41 minutes for rain (fit part; 5.4 hours and 40 minutes on validate). No rule could reach W7's 100 signalled city-days or its floor, so none is proposed, S2.3 and S2.4 are not run, and **the test part (2026-10-11 to 2026-10-22) stays unopened** for whatever uses it next (its first two days' trade files were opened only for the validate markets' last trades). The 99-cent maker lead and the 5-minute readings are parked in §9. KAPS's ingest keeps recording. |
| W8 | **Reported for every rule:**<ul><li>how long after a report counts the book moves past the bound (the competition's speed);</li><li>how often the ruled-out side traded after the bound (it should never happen; any case is studied);</li><li>the profit by hour of the local day.</li></ul>**No real order in S2.** A GO leads to a live shadow run (S2.4), then S1.3's write path on demo. |

## The build log

### S2.1 Sources, terms and the observation history (2026-10-27 to 2026-10-28, done)

**Built.** The package `sparq_analytics.weather`, run as `python -m sparq_analytics.weather stations|download|check|delay|delay-report`:
- `stations.py`: the map from climate site to station and zone (39 sites). Each market's site is read from its own rules (by code, or by station id). Markets naming no site take the one code their series and city use. Also the climate day (local standard time) with the civil day as a variant, and the scope: an explicit list of 24 daily-high series plus `KXRAIN`.
- `metar.py`: the parser.
  - Temperature from the tenths remark; otherwise from the body's whole degrees at the lower edge.
  - The six-hour maximum, and hourly precipitation, where a trace counts as 0.
  - Every temperature becomes its lowest possible whole °F (W4).
- `bounds.py`: each report's own readings and the day's running bound and rain flag, under both day definitions.
  - A six-hour maximum counts only when its six hours lie inside the day.
  - Rain counts only when the routine report it is measured from was inside the day.

- `check.py`: the gate.

  - It refuses any day in the test part.
- `live.py`: the delay recorder. It polls NOAA's aviation weather service once a minute; the first answer is the baseline.
- The two addresses and the user agent are in `sparq_core.config`; `httpx` is declared by `sparq-analytics` (already in the lock).

### S2.2 Exploration on the fit and validate parts (2026-10-28 UTC, done: no rules)

**Built.** `python -m sparq_analytics.weather explore --part fit|validate` (`weather/explore.py`). It refuses the test part by name and writes `explore-PART.json` and `explore-PART-trades.parquet` (the trades after each call) to the weather folder.
- *Timeline.* A report counts 10 minutes after its observation time (W4). A bound counts only where **both** kinds of climate day allow it: the lower of the standard and the civil day's running bound, and rain once both have it. A report near midnight can then never settle the wrong day. *Chosen in building (not in the slice plan):* S2.1 found no wrong call under either day, and this is the conservative way to use both.
- *The call:* the first counted moment the bound reaches the market's threshold (`check.threshold`, split out of `implied` so S2.1's and S2.2's calls cannot drift apart).
- *Ours:* a trade after the call whose taker bought the certain side, as approved. It is priced one cent worse (W5) with the taker fee rounded up to a cent, each trade one order, and paid on Kalshi's result (W2).
- *Capacity:* an upper bound (every such trade) and a lower bound (the first per market that was worth taking when it came: price and fee below a dollar), each with the profit per contract and a studentized bootstrap clustered by city-day.
