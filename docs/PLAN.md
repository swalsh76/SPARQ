## 1. Goal

SPARQ (Systematic Prediction-market Automated Risk-managed Quoting) makes money from prediction markets **as a maker**. On Kalshi the money flows from takers to makers (the 2026 makers-and-takers paper: takers -31.5% and makers -9.6% on average; ASSESSMENT §7), and the price itself is already close to the probability (KAPS's K0.5 study: NO-GO). So SPARQ does not try to out-predict the market. It:

- **quotes** at a **seconds** scale in **second-tier** markets, busy enough to trade but not the headline markets where professional firms quote in milliseconds;
- prices its quotes around a **fair value** from related markets (brackets, spreads against moneylines, one underlying across series) and from free or cheap outside anchors (spot prices, station observations, other venues);
- **never leaves a stale quote**: it refreshes, widens or pulls when its fair value or a related market moves, or when a market goes quiet;
- **takes other traders' stale quotes** when they are clearly wrong (at listing, after quiet spells, after a related market moves);
- stays **out** of headline markets and news races, which it would lose.

It anticipates what ordinary traders will do in the narrow sense that matters for a maker: when human-paced takers will arrive, and when an informed one is about to pick a quote off.

**Where the work stands against this goal (2026-11-02).** The goal above is unchanged, but the work has moved from it: since S2 every strand was chosen from data already held; the second tier was closed by S1; a fair value from related markets was measured only for taking stale quotes (S3.2), not for a maker's own quotes; outside anchors were measured only for weather (S2); and S4 to S6 work in the thin tail, priced off its own book. Whether to restate the goal and the gates (Q2, Q3) or to return to them is the owner's decision from the fresh-eyes review (BUILD-LOG).

## 3. Topology

As KAPS (KAPS PLAN §3), with one change of role: the **trading service on the analytics node is the quoter**. It holds the trading key, opens its own WebSocket connection for the markets it quotes, and reads related markets and outside anchors itself, so a database hop never sits between a price move and a quote change. The server host keeps KAPS's database and ingest; SPARQ reads from them and does not write to them (Q6).

## 5. The quoter's design (written for S1, which closed with D14; to be restated with the direction)

| Part | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Fair value** | From related Kalshi markets (bracket consistency, spreads against moneylines, one underlying across series), the book's size-weighted mid, and free or cheap outside anchors where a market has one. Each market type gets the simplest fair value that the evidence supports. |
| **Quoting** | Post-only quotes around the fair value in 100 to 150 second-tier markets, refreshed every 5 to 10 seconds; the price leans against the inventory it holds (Avellaneda and Stoikov's approach); quotes widen when a market goes quiet and are pulled when the fair value or a related market moves. |
| **Picking off** | Takes another trader's resting quote when it is off the fair value by more than the fee and a margin. |
| **Risk** | Limits per market, event, category and day; hedges in related markets (and perps where they exist); an expiry on every quote; a kill switch; an unclear order is looked up by `client_order_id`, never resent. |
| **Research loop** | Markouts after every fill (the price 5 seconds, 1 minute and 10 minutes later), profit split into spread earned, adverse selection, fees and inventory, and KAPS's study harness for offline tests. |

**Constraints known so far:** Kalshi's Advanced API tier allows about 30 writes a second (Basic about 10), enough for about 150 two-sided markets at a 10-second refresh; the order API supports post-only, `expiration_ts`, order groups, amend and batch (to be confirmed against the current API in S1). `/account/limits` and `/account/endpoint_costs` are read before the write path is designed.

### 6.4 S4 A paper quoter in the thin tail (approved 2026-10-29)

**Why this strand.** S3's one direction with money still worth measuring: in thin markets (under 1,000 contracts a day) the trades that cross a spread wider than 20 cents carry about $499,000 a day of half-spread, four fifths of it in Sports, with no quote within 10 cents of the mid to compete with (S3.3). That half-spread is gross revenue already earned by the wide makers resting at the touch, not money left unclaimed (corrected wording, the fresh-eyes review, 2026-11-02 (BUILD-LOG)). Everything else S3 measured is taken or closed: the 99-cent queue is rationed by the bots ahead of it (S3.1), and related markets answer within seconds (S3.2). What S3.3 could not say is what a maker standing inside those spreads would keep: how often its quote is hit and by whom (in the middle tier a late joiner is picked off, S1.2a), and what the position is worth afterwards in a market that may never trade again before it settles. S4 measures that on paper, sends no order, and builds nothing that trades.

### 6.5 S5 What a local language model can add: two measurements first (approved 2026-10-29)

**Why, and what to expect.** M6 promised the model node its own proposal. The evidence so far leaves a language model little to race: the price level is calibrated (K0.5), related markets answer a 2-cent move within seconds (S3.2), weather is priced hours before the public observation (S2.2), and the mid of a wide thin book is not where the market settles (S4's smoke run). A model at tens of tokens a second wins none of that. It could add two things: **reading rules text accurately at scale**, and **a probability for a question where the book is thin and its mid is a poor fair value**, which is exactly S4's gap. S5 measures those two on data already held, against bars set first, and builds nothing that trades. **My expectation, stated before reading: the reading measurement passes and the probability measurement does not.** The owner may reasonably park the node until a strand needs it; that option is in the questions below.

**What the endpoint is** (probed 2026-10-29 with three small synthetic prompts; no Kalshi data was sent). One model is served, a 3-bit quantized "flash" model of the Qwen family, not the 125B mixture-of-experts that PLAN §9 had inferred from Strata's description. It is a **reasoning model**: it writes its thinking first, and `content` comes back empty unless the token budget leaves room after it. It ran at 50 to 95 tokens a second. It read a weather rules text into the right fields, gave the right direction for two thresholds of one subject, and called a rain title a mismatch with its rules where a person would call it a match. Three prompts show only that the endpoint works; they are not a result.

### 6.6 S6 Recording the order books of the thin tail (approved 2026-11-02)

**Why this strand, and why now.** Every study so far has read the ingest's ticker feed: the best bid and ask and their sizes, never the queue behind them. A maker's real questions are about the queue: how much stands at a price, how long it stands, who joins and cancels, and where a new quote would rank. Book depth that is not recorded is lost; unlike trades it cannot be backfilled, so each day without a recorder is a day no later study can read. S1.2b's recorder was planned and approved on 2026-10-26 for the middle tier and parked unbuilt with S1 (D14). This proposal returns it, aimed at S4's thin tail.

**What it is not.** It is not a strategy and makes no claim of an edge. It is not S4.3's prerequisite: T9 defines the shadow quoter on the ingest's ticker tables. S4.2's smoke run (three fit-part days, not the test) marked S4's uncontested rules negative at settlement, so S4 may close with a NO-GO on 2026-11-13. The recorder's value does not rest on S4: it rests on the queue questions every maker direction in the thin tail meets. (The 99-cent queue parked in §9 lies outside B2's band and would need its own decision.)

## 7. Risks

- **Professional firms may move into second-tier markets,** and our place in the queue is unknown until real orders exist.
- **Retail flow may move.** Robinhood, reported to carry over half of Kalshi's volume, is building its own exchange with Susquehanna; if its flow leaves Kalshi, the spread paid in second-tier markets could shrink.
- **Rate limits and order capacity** are not yet measured for writes.
- **Adverse selection** (being filled just before the price moves against the quote) is the main cost; markouts measure it from the first shadow run.
- **Kalshi's Developer Agreement** (KAPS PLAN §7): own trading only; single user; storing and sharing data and public statements restricted; the points the owner set aside stay set aside.
- **This repository is public:** its quoting rules and any measured edge are visible to competitors (owner's choice, 2026-10-25).
- **Regulation:** prediction markets face state challenges, and the CFTC has scrutinised incentive programs; sports contracts are blocked in some states.
- **Key custody on macOS** for an unattended trading service (KAPS PLAN §7; designed with the write path, D7, §9).
